LoRaWAN vs Wi-SUN FAN for Smart Metering AMI — Which Protocol Wins?

Anand Shah, CTO, Nebulae 10 Jun 2026

With India's RDSS scheme mandating smart metering rollout across all DISCOMs and the Jal Jeevan Mission pushing water AMI, protocol selection has become the single most consequential decision in an AMI project. Choose the wrong one and you're looking at coverage holes, battery life shortfalls, or a compliance gap that delays go-live by months.

At Nebulae, we've deployed both LoRaWAN and Wi-SUN FAN across smart metering projects — water, electricity, and gas — in urban, peri-urban, and rural settings. This isn't a theoretical comparison. Here's what we've learned.

TL;DR: LoRaWAN wins on rural coverage and battery life. Wi-SUN wins on dense urban mesh and throughput. Both are RDSS and IS 16444 compliant. The decision comes down to your deployment topology.

LoRaWAN — Long Range, Star Topology, Battery King

LoRaWAN (Long Range Wide Area Network) uses Semtech's LoRa modulation at 865–867 MHz (IN865 band in India) to achieve 2–15 km range per gateway in a star-of-stars topology. Each meter node sends data directly to a gateway — no mesh, no routing, no relay.

The advantages for AMI are significant. A single gateway handles up to 10,000 meter nodes. Class A operation — where the node initiates all uplinks and only opens receive windows immediately after — allows devices to sleep for up to 15 minutes between transmissions, yielding 10-year AA battery life in production deployments.

  • Urban range: 2–5 km (dense buildings)
  • Rural range: 10–15 km (line of sight)
  • Battery life: 10 years on AA Li-SOCl₂ at 15-min intervals
  • Capacity: 10,000+ nodes per gateway
  • Downlink: Limited — 1% duty cycle means ~1 command per hour

Wi-SUN FAN — Mesh, High Throughput, Urban Champion

Wi-SUN FAN (Field Area Network) 1.1 operates at 865–867 MHz using IEEE 802.15.4g PHY with 6LoWPAN and RPL routing to create a self-healing mesh network. Every node can act as a router, extending the network's reach by hopping through neighbours.

In a dense apartment block in Mumbai, LoRaWAN failed to penetrate below the 3rd floor on 40% of units. Wi-SUN's mesh picked up every node through neighbour relaying. The topology changed everything.

Wi-SUN's mesh means there's no single gateway coverage radius to worry about — but it comes at a cost. Mesh routing burns more power. Battery life drops to 5–7 years. And the mesh needs density to work — sparse deployments with few nodes per area can leave nodes without neighbours to route through.

RDSS & IS 16444 Compliance

Both protocols are IS 16444 compliant for electricity AMI and IS 15500 compliant for water AMI in India. The Ministry of Power's RDSS scheme and AMISP guidelines do not mandate a specific RF protocol — they mandate the metering standard (IS 16444), communication security (AES-128 minimum), and data interface (DLMS/COSEM).

Nebulae modules for both LoRaWAN and Wi-SUN implement AES-128 at the MAC layer (LoRaWAN OTAA / Wi-SUN CCM*) and include a DLMS/COSEM push adapter for MDMS integration.

Our Recommendation by Deployment Type

Choose LoRaWAN if: Your deployment is rural, peri-urban, or a town with fewer than 50,000 connections. You need 10-year battery life. Your DISCOM wants a simple star topology with low gateway count.
Choose Wi-SUN if: Your deployment is dense urban (apartments, high-rises). You need a self-healing mesh for reliability. Throughput above 50 kbps matters (e.g., voltage quality logging, demand response). You're willing to trade battery life for coverage certainty.

Conclusion

The "LoRaWAN vs Wi-SUN" question has no universal answer — only a topology-specific one. At Nebulae, we've stopped asking which is better and started asking which fits this specific deployment. In practice, many large DISCOMs end up deploying LoRaWAN in rural feeders and Wi-SUN in dense urban zones — two networks, one MDMS, optimal protocol for each geography.

If you're in the pre-tender stage and need a coverage and capacity analysis for your specific feeder geography, our IoT Consulting team can run the link budget and recommend gateway placement before you commit to a protocol.
